### <strong>Fig. 3.</strong> Information cost is relational: binding axis and budget non-convexity.

![fig3](/home/marcelino/figures/interpretation/fig3_qBSeen.png)

<strong>Fig. 3.</strong> Frequency of cooperators under different information-cost assignments and cooperation mechanisms. (<strong>a–c</strong>) When short-memory partner choice is the only cooperation mechanism allowed. (<strong>d–f</strong>) When partner choice and indirect reciprocity are both allowed. (<strong>a, d</strong>) Information cost falls only on the population with the lower cooperation cost (i₁ = 0, i₀ swept; orange line: population with cooperation cost c₀ = 0.1; red line: population with cooperation cost c₁ = 0.2). (<strong>b, e</strong>) Information cost falls only on the population with the higher cooperation cost (i₀ = 0, i₁ swept; orange line: population with cooperation cost c₀ = 0.1; red line: population with cooperation cost c₁ = 0.2). (<strong>c, f</strong>) Total information cost is held at i₀ + i₁ = 0.2 while the split between the populations varies (orange line: population with cooperation cost c₀ = 0.1; red line: population with cooperation cost c₁ = 0.2). The two populations have cooperation cost <em>c<sub>0</sub></em> (0.1) and <em>c<sub>1</sub></em> (0.2).

Under partner choice (a, b) both populations are nearly flat in their own cost and collapse under the partner's — assortment needs an active chooser on each side and is limited by whichever supplies fewer. Under the combined reputation-rich mechanism (d, e) the binding axis swaps: taxing the low-cooperation-cost population to i = 0.20 drives its partner from 0.957 to 0.268 while the payer falls only to 0.734. Common x-range on those strips is the tighter per-population cap b − cₚ. Under the combined mechanism (f) both populations reach their lowest cooperation at an interior split rather than at either end: each extreme leaves one intact apparatus, enough to hold the pair together, whereas a split breaks both. Partner choice (c) is monotone instead — only the high-cooperation-cost population's information cost bites — so the non-convexity is a property of reciprocity-bearing mechanisms, not of the budget. Full i₀ × i₁ coverage is in Fig. S19. Snowdrift counterpart: Fig. S20. Fitness counterpart: Fig. S15.

### <strong>Fig. 4.</strong> Role inversion appears only when the cheap population's information cost is near zero.

![fig4](/home/marcelino/figures/interpretation/fig4_qBSeen.png)

<strong>Fig. 4.</strong> Frequency of cooperators and average fitness under different information costs on the cheap population. (<strong>a–d</strong>) Frequency of cooperators. (<strong>e–h</strong>) Average fitness. (<strong>a, e</strong>) Information cost on the lower-cooperation-cost population is held at i₀ = 0 while i₁ is swept (orange line: population with cooperation cost c₀ = 0.1; red line: population with cooperation cost c₁ = 0.2). (<strong>b, f</strong>) Information cost on the lower-cooperation-cost population is held at i₀ = 0.02 while i₁ is swept (orange line: population with cooperation cost c₀ = 0.1; red line: population with cooperation cost c₁ = 0.2). (<strong>c, g</strong>) Information cost on the lower-cooperation-cost population is held at i₀ = 0.04 while i₁ is swept (orange line: population with cooperation cost c₀ = 0.1; red line: population with cooperation cost c₁ = 0.2). (<strong>d, h</strong>) Information cost on the lower-cooperation-cost population is held at i₀ = 0.1 while i₁ is swept (orange line: population with cooperation cost c₀ = 0.1; red line: population with cooperation cost c₁ = 0.2). The two populations have cooperation cost <em>c<sub>0</sub></em> (0.1) and <em>c<sub>1</sub></em> (0.2).

The cooperation inversion — the expensive population's curve above the cheap one — holds throughout when i₀ = 0, survives only past a threshold when i₀ = 0.02, and is gone by i₀ = 0.04 and 0.1. Shaded ±1 SD bands over 30 runs mark a bistable threshold: wide below it (runs split between attractors) and collapsed above it. Fitness stays decoupled from cooperation in the inversion regime: the expensive population can cooperate more while remaining less fit. Snowdrift removes this regime (Fig. S21). The equal-c hitchhiking contrast is Fig. S17 (snowdrift: Fig. S18). Full i₀ × i₁ coverage behind these strips is Fig. S19.
