### <strong>Fig. 1.</strong> Outcome asymmetry under parameter-symmetric cooperation cost (partner choice).

![fig1](/home/marcelino/figures/interpretation/fig1.png)

<strong>Fig. 1.</strong> Frequency of cooperators and average fitness in two coevolving populations (light green: lower frequency of cooperators; dark green: the other). (<strong>a–d</strong>) Prisoner's dilemma. (<strong>e–h</strong>) Snowdrift. (<strong>a, e</strong>) Frequency of cooperators without enforcement (_). (<strong>b, f</strong>) Frequency of cooperators under short-memory partner choice (P; last-act ranking, no Q). (<strong>c, g</strong>) Fitness without enforcement. (<strong>d, h</strong>) Fitness under partner choice. Both populations pay identical cooperation costs (<em>c</em>, from 0 to <em>b</em>) and identical information costs (0.001).

With matched costs (c₀ = c₁), partner choice produces a stochastic cooperator/exploiter assignment in the prisoner's dilemma that is absent without enforcement (a–d); the cooperating side can earn lower fitness (paradox of success). In snowdrift the same split already appears in the no-enforcement columns; partner choice mainly extends that split to higher costs (e–h). Fig. 2 converts the chance split into a deterministic one; Fig. S5 contrasts the two strips on shared axes.

### <strong>Fig. 2.</strong> Deterministic outcome split under cooperation-cost parameter asymmetry.

![fig2](/home/marcelino/figures/interpretation/fig2.png)

<strong>Fig. 2.</strong> Frequency of cooperators and average fitness for two coevolving populations under the prisoner's dilemma. (<strong>a, b</strong>) Short-memory partner choice (P). (<strong>c, d</strong>) Combined mechanism IJMPQ (partner choice on recent and lifetime cooperation plus direct and indirect reciprocity). Both populations pay identical information costs (0.001). Strip c₁ = c₀ + 0.02 with both populations overlaid.

Along c₁ = c₀ + 0.02 with both populations overlaid, partner choice converts the R − P gap into a stable cooperator/exploiter assignment (a, b); combined IJMPQ raises cooperation in the expensive population and shrinks the outcome asymmetry (c, d). Prisoner's dilemma only: under no enforcement the cheap population barely cooperates (Fig. S3), so partner choice creates the deterministic split from the parameter gap. In snowdrift the sucker payoff already sustains high cooperation without mechanism alleles (Fig. 1e–h), so a parallel asymmetric panel would repeat that already-high cooperation. Full c₀ × c₁ coverage is in Fig. S4; the split survives group size 4 (Fig. S6).

### <strong>Fig. 3.</strong> Cooperation persists after enforcement alleles are lost where cooperation cost is zero.

![fig3](/home/marcelino/figures/interpretation/fig3.png)

<strong>Fig. 3.</strong> Frequency of the enforcement allele (P1 in a; M1 in c) and frequency of cooperators (b, d) in a single population. (<strong>a, b</strong>) Short-memory partner choice (P). (<strong>c, d</strong>) Direct reciprocity (M). Cooperation cost fixed at 0; information cost swept from 0 to <em>b</em> (0.4).

At c = 0, information cost selects out active enforcement alleles (chooser P1 in a; tit-for-tat M1 in c) while cooperation remains high on unconditional cooperators that pay no information cost (b, d). Cooperation frequency and mechanism-allele frequency diverge. The full information-cost × cooperation-cost grid is in Fig. S7; Fig. S8 shows that mechanism alleles decline in the prisoner's dilemma and in the control game, but cooperation persists after those alleles are lost only when the dilemma is present. That pattern is what Figs. 4–5 price: relieving the payer removes the assortment or conditional help its partner depends on.

### <strong>Fig. 4.</strong> Information cost is relational: which population's cost binds, and shared totals are not additive.

![fig4](/home/marcelino/figures/interpretation/fig4_qBSeen.png)

<strong>Fig. 4.</strong> Frequency of cooperators in two coevolving populations under the prisoner's dilemma. (<strong>a–c</strong>) Short-memory partner choice (P). (<strong>d–f</strong>) Combined mechanism IJMPQ. (<strong>a, d</strong>) Information cost only on the low-cooperation-cost population (i₁ = 0, i₀ swept; orange: c₀ = 0.1; red: c₁ = 0.2). (<strong>b, e</strong>) Information cost only on the high-cooperation-cost population (i₀ = 0, i₁ swept). (<strong>c, f</strong>) Total information cost fixed at i₀ + i₁ = 0.2 while the split varies. Cooperation costs c₀ = 0.1 and c₁ = 0.2.

Each of the first two columns holds one population's information cost at zero and sweeps the other's, separating the cost a population pays from the cost its partner pays. Under partner choice (a, b) both populations are nearly flat in their own cost and cooperate much less under the partner's — assortment needs an active chooser on each side and is limited by whichever supplies fewer. Under IJMPQ (d, e) the pattern reverses: raising information cost on the low-cooperation-cost population to i = 0.20 drives its partner from 0.957 to 0.268 while the payer falls only to 0.734. Common x-range on those strips is the tighter per-population cap b − cₚ. The third column holds total information cost at 0.2 while the split varies, so every point costs the pair the same. Under IJMPQ (f) both populations reach their lowest cooperation at an interior split rather than at either end: each extreme leaves one population still carrying mechanism alleles, enough to sustain cooperation in both, whereas a split reduces both. Partner choice (c) is monotone instead — only the high-cooperation-cost population's information cost matters — so the non-additivity is a property of reciprocity-bearing mechanisms, not of the total.

### <strong>Fig. 5.</strong> Role inversion appears only when the cheap population's information cost is near zero.

![fig5](/home/marcelino/figures/interpretation/fig5_qBSeen.png)

<strong>Fig. 5.</strong> Frequency of cooperators when IJMPQ is allowed. (<strong>a–h</strong>) Two coevolving populations. Prisoner's dilemma. (<strong>a, e</strong>) i₀ = 0 while i₁ is swept (orange: c₀ = 0.1; red: c₁ = 0.2). (<strong>b, f</strong>) i₀ = 0.02. (<strong>c, g</strong>) i₀ = 0.04. (<strong>d, h</strong>) i₀ = 0.1. Cooperation costs c₀ = 0.1 and c₁ = 0.2.

Top row: cooperation. Bottom row: fitness. Each column fixes i₀ on the low-cooperation-cost population and sweeps i₁. The cooperation inversion — the expensive population's curve above the cheap one — holds throughout when i₀ = 0, survives only past a threshold when i₀ = 0.02, and is gone by i₀ = 0.04 and 0.1. Shaded ±1 SD bands over 30 runs mark a bistable threshold: wide below it (runs diverge to different outcomes) and narrow above it. Fitness stays separated from cooperation in the inversion regime: the expensive population can cooperate more while remaining less fit. Snowdrift removes this regime. The equal-c role-inversion contrast is Fig. S11.
