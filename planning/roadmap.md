## Framing decisions (locked 2026-07)

- **Central contribution:** the burden created by enforcement cost often does **not**
  stay with the payer. Which population matters most depends on the mechanism family.
  Behaviour–mechanism decoupling is the process, not the headline.
- **Presentation:** single central claim; sweep axes as boundary conditions in main text
  + supplement.
- **Active venue:** The American Naturalist (Major Article). Submission files:
  [../paper/submission/amnat/](../paper/submission/amnat/instructions.md).
- **Scope:** unified article — cooperation-cost baselines plus full information-cost
  programme; calibration sweeps auxiliary (journal only).
- **Retired split:** separate papers for information cost vs two-population asymmetry.

## Narrative arc

1. Enforcement mechanisms; models treat machinery as free.
2. Mechanism hierarchy and two-population role split (cooperation-cost asymmetry).
3. Costly machinery and decoupling; symmetric design cannot separate payer from burden.
4. **Headline:** per-population information cost; cross-population burden by mechanism.
5. Budget non-convexity; shedding as destructive under asymmetry.
6. Boundary conditions: dilemma structure, near-zero-i₀, snowdrift.
